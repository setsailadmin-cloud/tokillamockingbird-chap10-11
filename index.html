Homework Essay Prompt

"In Chapters 10 and 11, Atticus Finch subverts the children's superficial understanding of power and heroism—contrasting physical skill with moral endurance. Select one key symbol or episode (The Mockingbird Doctrine, Tim Johnson the Rabid Dog, or Mrs. Dubose's White Camellia) and write a 150–300 word analytical paragraph explaining how Harper Lee uses it to redefine 'courage' and integrity."

Assignment Criteria:

  • Length: 150 to 300 words.
  • Thesis: Identify chosen symbol & its thematic significance.
  • Text Evidence: Integrate at least one direct quote from Chapter 10 or 11.
  • Depth: Contrast physical power (marksmanship) with moral courage.

3 Key Symbolic Angles for In-Class Discussion

Discussion Guide
Symbol 1 Chapter 10

The Mockingbird ("It's a Sin to Kill a Mockingbird")

Core Meaning: Total innocence and harmlessness. Unlike destructive pests, mockingbirds only provide beautiful music. Harming them is an unprovoked violation of moral decency.

Discussion Talking Points:
  • Why is Atticus's admonition about mockingbirds the only time Scout ever hears him say something is a "sin"?
  • How does Miss Maudie elaborate upon this rule?
  • Which characters in Maycomb function as metaphorical mockingbirds (e.g., Boo Radley, Tom Robinson)?
Symbol 2 Chapter 10

Tim Johnson & "Ol' One-Shot Finch" (Power vs. Restraint)

Core Meaning: The mad dog symbolizes Maycomb's latent racism and irrational hysteria ("Maycomb's usual disease"). Atticus's lethal marksmanship demonstrates that true strength is exercised with reluctance and humility.

Discussion Talking Points:
  • Why did Atticus keep his shooting prowess hidden from his children for years?
  • What does Miss Maudie mean when she explains that Atticus realized his marksmanship gave him an unfair advantage over living creatures?
  • Why does Jem passionately declare "Atticus is a gentleman, just like me!" and command Scout not to boast at school?
Symbol 3 Chapter 11

The White Camellia ("Snow-on-the-Mountain") & Real Courage

Core Meaning: Moral victory, forgiveness, and freedom. Mrs. Dubose conquers her morphine addiction to die "beholden to nothing and nobody," leaving Jem a pristine white bloom.

Discussion Talking Points:
  • How does Atticus define "real courage" to Jem? ("...when you know you're licked before you begin but you begin anyway and you see it through no matter what.")
  • How does Mrs. Dubose's struggle mirror Atticus's unwinnable defense of Tom Robinson?
  • Why does Jem initially fling the flower down, and why is he found quietly caressing its petals that night?

Exemplar Analytical Response (221 Words): Theme: Redefining Real Courage (Ch. 11)

In Chapter 11 of To Kill a Mockingbird, Harper Lee uses the grueling ordeal of Mrs. Henry Lafayette Dubose to radically redefine the children's conception of courage. Prior to this chapter, Jem and Scout perceive strength as physical dominance, admiring Atticus for felling the rabid dog Tim Johnson with a single rifle shot. However, Atticus intentionally sentences Jem to read to the cantankerous Mrs. Dubose so the boy can observe a far deeper brand of fortitude. Despite knowing that she will die within weeks, Mrs. Dubose endures agonizing withdrawal symptoms to break her morphine addiction, determined to leave the world "beholden to nothing and nobody." Following her passing, Atticus articulates the core thematic thesis of the novel: "I wanted you to see what real courage is, instead of getting the idea that courage is a man with a gun in his hand. It's when you know you're licked before you begin but you begin anyway and you see it through no matter what." By bequeathing Jem the pristine white camellia ("Snow-on-the-Mountain"), Mrs. Dubose extends unspoken reconciliation and proof of her spiritual victory. Through this encounter, Lee underscores that genuine nobility lies not in dominating weapons, but in the unwavering resolution to fight unwinnable moral battles with integrity.
